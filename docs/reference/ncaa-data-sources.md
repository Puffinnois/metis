# NCAA Division I data sources: what was measured

Measured 2026-11-18 (Plan T1302.1) against the men's and women's college basketball releases of [sportsdataverse-data](https://github.com/sportsdataverse/sportsdataverse-data). The asset list was read whole through the GitHub API. 1,072 of the 1,244 Parquet files were downloaded (1,377 MB): every file outside the play, shot, possession, lineup and stint releases, and a first, middle and last season of each of those. The other 172 were read for their row counts and schemas from their Parquet footers over HTTP range requests, and their game ids were read the same way, a column at a time, without downloading the files. Everything was read with DuckDB 1.5.6 (`@duckdb/node-api`). The 2025-26 season (`2026`) is complete; 2026-27 (`2027`) had started on 2026-11-01. Re-measure before relying on these numbers much later. The [WNBA's document](wnba-data-sources.md) is the model, and the owner's research, [ncaa-d1-basketball-statistics.md](ncaa-d1-basketball-statistics.md), is the catalogue of statistics this document checks against the data.

The raw files are kept outside the repository, in `~/.cache/metis-dev/ncaa-raw` (one folder per release). Beside them are the asset list (`~/.cache/metis-dev/bb-assets.tsv`, every basketball asset with its size), the release list (`sdv-releases.tsv`), every Parquet file's rows and columns (`ncaa-files.tsv`), the column survey (`ncaa-schema.json`), and the NCAA pages and PDFs read with a plain client (`ncaa-probe/`, the PDFs as text beside them).

"M" is the men (NCAAM), "W" the women (NCAAW). Seasons are labelled by their final year, as in the data (`2026` = 2025-26).

**The window: 2021-22 on.** The owner decided on 2026-11-18 that the NCAA dataset starts at the 2021-22 season (`2022`) and runs to now, for men and women alike (D39). The tables below still list every season the releases offer, so the coverage shows what the source holds. The load, the agreement between the families and the choice of source per table are measured for 2022 to 2027. Traps of earlier seasons get a line each at the end of "Traps". Three 2022 play-by-play files (175 MB) were downloaded for this after the decision, so 1,075 files and 1,552 MB are kept.

## The source

Three families, each with a men's and a women's release:

| Family         | Releases                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Upstream                                                                                                | Seasons (M / W)       |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| ESPN           | `espn_mens_college_basketball_*`, `espn_womens_college_basketball_*`: schedules, player and team box scores, season stats, standings, play by play, shots, game rosters, rosters, player bios (`player_core`), officials (12 releases a side)              | ESPN's men's and women's college basketball APIs ("Upstream source: ESPN men's-college-basketball API") | 2002–2027 / 2001–2027 |
| stats.ncaa.org | `ncaa_mbb_*`, `ncaa_wbb_*`: schedule, team ids, rosters, team rosters, player and team box scores, play by play, possessions, lineups, matchup stints, shots, RAPM, RAPM within team (13 releases a side)                                                  | "stats.ncaa.org official box scores and play-by-play", the NCAA's own statistics site                   | 2010–2027 / 2010–2027 |
| The dataset's  | `mbb_crosswalk`, `wbb_crosswalk` (ids across ESPN, stats.ncaa.org, Fox, Yahoo, KenPom and Torvik), `mbb_groups`, `wbb_groups` (conferences by season), `mbb_ratings`, `wbb_ratings` (team ratings), `mbb_player_value`, `wbb_player_value` (box-score BPM) | ESPN and NCAA inputs, "scored through the hoopR model stack" (the women's: wehoop)                      | 2002–2027 / 2002–2027 |

**The stats.ncaa.org family is the finding that changes the plan.** The research expected hoopR's and wehoop's ESPN releases. The releases also hold the NCAA's own box scores and play by play for every Division I game from 2010-11, with derived possessions, lineups and stints, and the dataset's RAPM. In the window both families cover every game, and they agree closely ("Which source each table reads"). Before it, stats.ncaa.org was the women's only complete source of player lines until 2016-17. Its release notes say "stats.ncaa.org bans aggressive clients, so capture runs from the SportsDataverse droplet under a rate budget". Metis reads only the release files: it makes no request to stats.ncaa.org, ncaa.com or ESPN, as D7 has it for the NBA's stats site, and nothing in the coverage, repeats or agreement sections came from them. The pages read for the check are listed under "What the check can compare against".

**`data-rights.md`** needs an NCAA entry before the league ships (T1302.2 below). Its facts: the ESPN releases are under CC BY 4.0, as the NBA's and the WNBA's producers are. The stats.ncaa.org releases state no licence. Their upstream, stats.ncaa.org, refuses plain clients (HTTP 403, below), and the producer captures it under a rate budget, which is the same position as the NBA's `nba_stats_*` releases from stats.nba.com. The NCAA's and ESPN's own terms were not read for this part. D41 decides it: they load on the same footing as the `nba_stats_*` releases under D31, and each league can ship and run without them on ESPN's tables alone.

## Size

The whole asset list, read through the GitHub API: **58 basketball releases (29 a side), 1,244 Parquet files, 5,677 MB.** The same data is also published as CSV (748 files, 33,209 MB), RDS (1,097 files, 6,749 MB) and gzipped CSV (444 files, 4,841 MB), which Metis ignores, and 230 small JSON and text files (the model cards, `timestamp` and `package_function` sidecars).

Each side is twice the NBA's 1,554 MB, and the men are ten times the WNBA's 296 MB.

**The window, 2022 to 2027**, with the files that hold every season: 192 men's files, 1,147 MB, and 191 women's, 1,085 MB. The four files that hold every season are 56.5 MB: ESPN's two schedule masters and two `games_in_data_repo` files, whose rows before 2022 would be left out. stats.ncaa.org is 665 and 641 MB of it, ESPN's play by play 329 and 322 MB.

### Teams

Division I teams per season, from the groups' ESPN team ids: men 327 (2002) to 365 (2026, 2027); women 314 (2002) to 363 (2026, 2027). ESPN's schedules hold many more teams, since they list every opponent: 727 men's teams have a player line in 2026, and 663 women's. ### Shots

stats.ncaa.org's shot file gives every shot a location in feet from the basket (`shot_x` across, −25 to 25; `shot_y` along, from −5; `dist_ft` their distance), so the shot charts' tenths are ten times them; its own zones (`shot_zone`: rim under 4 feet, paint 4 to 15, mid-range, corner and above-the-break threes) are radial, not the lane, and are kept as each shot's `action`. Metis classifies the zones from the coordinates and the published value, as for the NBA, with the college lane of 12 feet (`League.laneHalfWidth`, the NBA's is 16). It gives **no shot type** (`shot_type` is `unknown` on every row of the window), **no assist**, and a period and clock **only from 2026-27**; so the counts by type read "stats.ncaa.org's shot file gives no shot type.", assisted makes by zone read their reason (the play by play names the assist, but a shot has no clock to find its play before 2026-27; assisted twos and threes are counted from the plays), and a chart orders a game's shots without a clock. About 5 % of shots have no shooter id (players of schools outside Division I, 33,602 men's in 2025-26): they carry a code of two letters of the first name and the last name (`AuPace`), matched to `ncaa_player_ids` within the team; 99.4 % of the men's 2025-26 shots are credited to an ESPN athlete and the rest to D28's keys.

Against the box score (exact, per team-game): the shots' attempts equal ESPN's team box on 96.9 % (men) and 96.4 % (women) of 2021-22's team-games, rising to 99.8 % and 99.5 % in 2025-26, and stats.ncaa.org's own box on the same share; three-point attempts on fewer, 86.0 to 95.5 %, so the file's point values differ from the box's on a few percent of threes. **ESPN's shots beside them** (`shot_espn`), its made free throws flagged and never attempts (196,761 to 253,492 men's a season): their attempts equal stats.ncaa.org's on 79.7 % of men's team-games in 2021-22 and 98.9 % in 2025-26, and ESPN locates 4 to 26 % of its field goals before 2025-26 and 99.6 % from it.

**The three-point line.** The rule is 22 feet 1¾ inches on the arc and 21 feet 7⅞ inches in the corners (the NCAA's rules texts, D38's addition), and the registry and the drawn court carry it. A shot's value and zone are the file's own `point_value` (every shot has one), never a comparison of its coordinates with the line. Where the value flips, measured on the located shots of every season of both leagues: on the arc the farthest two is 22.15 feet and the nearest three 22.26, the rule; in the corners (within 4.7 feet of the basket along the court) the widest two is 20.5 feet across and the nearest three 21.0, on the file's half-foot grid, with 1.0 to 1.7 % of corner threes inside 21.65 feet and the rest beyond: charting scatter, not another line.

### Lineups, stints and possessions

## The plays, built (T1302.7)

Built on 2026-11-19 on fresh copies of `t1302f-ncaam` and `t1302f-ncaaw` through the built worker's refresh (`scripts/load-report.mjs`, the app's 1 GB DuckDB limit and two threads): 8.9 minutes (men) and 7.8 (women), 2.75 and 2.76 GB peak memory, every season of the window rebuilt (COLLEGE_PLAYS 4). The checks are in [ncaa-agreement.md](ncaa-agreement.md), "The plays (T1302.7)". stats.ncaa.org's play by play is the source (D42: ESPN's is not loaded); with its releases left out (D41) the tables are empty and every play-built metric and split gives "Not included in this version." (tested).

**How the feed is read** (`src/main/stats/derived/collegePbp.ts`), measured on every season of both leagues:

- **No play number.** The file's rows are in play order within a game, and the load keeps that order within each of DuckDB's row groups but not between them (`preserve_insertion_order` is off): 22 of the men's 6,297 games of 2025-26 are not contiguous by `rowid`, 11 of them in two parts the wrong way round. A play's `seq` is its place by period, the file's `game_seconds`, then `rowid`.
- **Sides.** stats.ncaa.org's home and away are its own; its home team is placed by its stats.ncaa.org id, ESPN id, then name, and the ten follow. In 62 men's games of 2025-26 its score columns run the other way from its home and away names (its teams and players placed right): each column is read as the side whose points it tracks, so every game ends on its final score (99.9 to 100 % a season; the rest are games the file stops short).
- **Players** through `ncaa_player_ids` by the file's id, else the negative of that id, else the play-by-play name (`AJ.DYBANTSA`) within the team, else D28's key, as the `possession` view keys them.
- **Event names.** Every `event_type` and what it maps to is listed with its count in ncaa-agreement.md ("The event names"): field goals (Three Point Jumper, Two Point Jumper, Layup, Dunk, Hook, and the older format's Tip In, Unk and empty types with "2PTR"), Free Throw, Offensive, Defensive and Deadball Rebound (a dead-ball rebound counts for no one, a team's own rebound for the team), Turnover, Commits Foul, Steal, Blocked Shot, the jump balls, substitutions, timeouts and challenges. Draw Foul (1,064,966 men's, 995,129 women's rows) names the fouled player and is kept as `other` for the fouls drawn. In 2021-22 to 2024-25, 474 men's and 278 women's rows carry the player's name or "null" before the type ("PIERCE,D'#393aAUNTRAY Enters Game") and are read without it. Unmapped: 594 of 15,299,464 men's rows and 187 of 14,760,049 women's (under 0.01 %), mostly empty types of the older format ("FOUL TE", "STARTER") and seven "Wrong Basket" rows.
- **Older format.** 2021-22 to 2023-24 hold a few hundred games (161, 88 and 39 men's) in stats.ncaa.org's older text ("LEDLUM,CHRIS made Layup - KENNEDY,MARQUISE Assist"), where rows of one second come in any order (an offensive rebound before its miss) and a free throw sometimes before its foul. Their results come from the description where `event_result` is empty.
- **Steals and blocks** are rows of their own at the same second as the turnover or miss (every steal of 2025-26, 99.8 % of blocks): each goes on the nearest such row of the other team, as the NBA's do.
- **Assists** are the make's `player_2`. **Personal fouls** count a player's technical (the box score does: 99.5 % of men's player-games with it, 98.2 % without) and leave out administrative technicals on a coach or the bench.
- **Kinds.** Turnover types (`badpass`, `lostball`, `offensive`, `travel`, `shotclock`, `outofbounds`, and `3sec`, `5sec`, `10sec`, `dribbling`, `laneviolation`, `offensivegoaltending` as other violations) and foul classes (`personal`, shooting, `offensive`, loose ball, technical, flagrant) map to the play-derived kinds (`PlayKind.ncaa`); 0.7 to 3.9 % of turnovers (`other` or none in the file) and 0.7 to 4.2 % of fouls (technicals, flagrants) are of no kind. The feed records no ejection, defensive three seconds, goaltending or kicked-ball violation, and no take, clear-path or flagrant type: those metrics give their reason (`catalogue/college.ts`).
- **Periods.** The men's two halves and the women's four quarters are the registry's; the splits read halves (men) and quarters (women, their halves the quarters added up) and overtimes. The file writes no row at a period's start or end (the tip is at 19:59, the last play at 0:02 or 0:05): the splits' time and the play-credited units give those seconds to the ten of the period's first and last play. Clutch is the NBA's definition, the last five minutes of the final regulation period or an overtime within five points (`SPLITS` in `shared/stats/splits.ts`, the second half in a game of halves): the NCAA publishes none, and on the 40-minute game it is the same stretch for the men's halves and the women's quarters.

## Traps

- **A season is labelled by its final year** in both families (`2026` = 2025-26, games from 2025-11-03 to 2026-04-06), as for the NBA. Screens show `2025-26`. stats.ncaa.org's own sport-year ids and Sportradar's seasons count from the first year (the research's section 17); none of that reaches the releases.
- **ESPN's schedule files hold each game once** (corrected at T1302.3). T1302.1 wrote that almost every game had two rows; counted again on the raw files and the first load, every season file has as many rows as games: 4,990 for 4,990 men's games in 2003, 6,318 for 6,318 in 2026, the women's alike in every season sampled (2003, 2010, 2015, 2019, 2021 to 2027). The earlier count, 136,773 men's games with two rows, is about the master's 136,915 games, so it most likely counted the season files and the master together. The season files from 2021-22 hold the master's games to 2025-26 and the current season's whole schedule (6,074 men's and 5,804 women's games on 2026-11-18, most not yet played). The schedule master holds every game of the season files to 2025-26 again, row for row: the two copies a game has, of which the season file is read (T1302.4).
- **Season types: ESPN's `season_type` is 2 or 3, never 1.** Conference tournaments are regular season (type 2): marked by `tournament_id` with `conference_competition` (300 men's and 324 women's games in 2026), and by `notes_headline` ("Big Ten Tournament - Final", "SEC Tournament", "Atlantic 10 Championship"). Early-season events are type 2 as well, with a `tournament_id` and a headline ("Players Era Festival", "Battle 4 Atlantis"). Type 3 holds the NCAA tournament and the other postseason events. No exhibition appears: there is no type 1, and the NCAA's policies say exhibitions do not count. ESPN gives early-season events a `tournament_id` from 2023-24 only: in 2021-22 and 2022-23 every type 2 game with one is a conference tournament game.
- **The NCAA tournament's headline changes by era**: region names ("EAST REGIONAL AT DAYTON OH", "FINAL FOUR") in 2002–2007, "MEN'S BASKETBALL CHAMPIONSHIP" in 2008–2020, "Men's Basketball Championship" in 2021–2025, "NCAA Men's Basketball Championship - …" in 2026 (`tournament_id` 22). Classified by headline, it is 63 or 64 games to 2010 and 67 from 2011 for the men (66 in 2021), and 63 for the women to 2021 and 67 from 2022. Read the event from type 3 with an era rule, never from one spelling.
- **The other postseason events, all type 3**: men's NIT (31 games a season from 2007 by headline, but 23 in 2017 and 16 in 2021; 39–40 before), CBI (from 2008), CIT (2009–2019 and 2024), Vegas 16 (2016), The Basketball Classic (2022), College Basketball Crown (2025–2026); women's WNIT (31 to 63 games), WBI (2010–2023), WBIT (2024–2026). In the NCAA's own statistics, "postseason contests including conference and/or NCAA tournaments … shall be included" (Statistics Policies), so a season total counts them all. Each event is a season type of its own, for filtering.
- **Non-Division I opponents are in the data.** ESPN lists every opponent (727 men's teams with a player line in 2026), and marks a team outside Division I with `is_active` false: 548 of the 548 men's 2026 games against a team missing from the groups, 405 of 407 women's. The groups file (`*_team_group_seasons`) is the season's Division I list. Players of those teams have ESPN box-score lines (a leaderboard that keeps them ranks one-game players first). The NCAA counts games against "countable" opponents only, and its list of non-countable ones (on ncaa.org) was not read, so the data cannot tell a countable non-Division I opponent from a non-countable one: Metis would count every game a Division I team played, and keep non-Division I teams and players out of the leaderboards and the ranks. Built (T1302.4): 322 to 387 men's and 215 to 322 women's teams outside Division I a season, kept as teams with `division_i` false. stats.ncaa.org also lists games against schools outside Division I that ESPN does not list at all: 11 in the window, against teams no source gives an ESPN id (D28 keys them by name). Seven are before opening day, exhibitions, kept as `preseason` ("Exhibition") and never counted; four are later and counted. Sports Reference counts one of the eleven (Alabama State against Tuskegee). D45's "no exhibitions in the data" holds for ESPN only (amended 2026-11-18). See "Games, teams and players, built".
- **ESPN's 2025 women's schedule is short**: 5,648 games against 5,923 in 2024, 6,054 in 2026 and stats.ncaa.org's 5,960 for 2025. ESPN lacks 334 games stats.ncaa.org has (T1302.4), most of them conference games and tournaments of February and March, 16 postseason (11 WNIT, 5 WBIT). The game crosswalk gives each an ESPN id, which ESPN's files lack; the game table keys them by it and fills them from stats.ncaa.org.
- **stats.ncaa.org's player ids are per season.** None of 81,287 men's and 74,454 women's player ids appears in more than one season. A player across seasons, and across a transfer, is ESPN's `athlete_id`, joined through the player crosswalk's `ncaa_player_id` (92–99 % of ESPN's rows from 2010). 8–12 % of stats.ncaa.org's lines in the window have no ESPN athlete in the crosswalk (14,721 of 127,465 men's in 2026), so they need box-score matching or D28's keys. The RAPM file has a `person_id` beside `player_id`, not examined. Measured at T1302.4: the misses are mostly lines with no player id (6,300 to 9,500 a season, mostly players of teams outside Division I) and players the 2025-26 crosswalk lacks; matched by box score and name, 0.13 to 0.86 % of the lines are left with D28's keys.
- **stats.ncaa.org's team ids are per season too**: Texas A&M-Corpus Christi is 51431 in 2009-10 and 609609 in 2025-26. `ncaa_espn_team_crosswalk` maps each season's id to ESPN's `team_id`, which is the same across seasons, with the school's `ncaa_org_id`.
- **The two families' ids for a game are sometimes wrong** (T1302.4): the game crosswalk gives Hawaii's game with Hawaii Hilo of 2021-11-11 the ESPN id of Hawaii's game with Northern Colorado that day, and stats.ncaa.org's team box gives Hawaii Hilo Northern Colorado's ESPN team id; a few contests give one side's ESPN id wrong, or the same id to both. Link by rank, with the score, never by the id alone.
- **A postponed game keeps its first date in stats.ncaa.org**: Towson against Delaware, 69-57, is 2022-01-27 there and 2022-02-28 in ESPN's schedule. Two teams and a score in the same season tell it.
- **stats.ncaa.org lists a contest twice** at least once: Iowa against Virginia Tech of 2026-11-10, 88-93 and 89-93 under two contest ids.
- **The two families' scores differ by a point or two on about one game in a thousand** (5 men's and 7 women's linked games in 2021-22, none in 2023-24 and 2025-26).
- **ESPN files a forfeit as a played 2-0 game** (`STATUS_FORFEIT`): men 10 in 2022 and 6 in 2023, women 22, 8, 10, 3 and 1 from 2022 to 2026. Sports Reference counts two of them (Kansas City's and St. Thomas's of 2024-25, women). The game table keeps them as played, with `forfeit_winner_id`, as the WNBA's 2018 forfeit; a record that leaves them out reads that column.
- **The team crosswalk's Sports Reference slug is filled in 2026-27 only**, and a few of its slugs are not Sports Reference's own (BYU's): match the school pages by slug where the page has it, else by name.
- **Players across transfers.** ESPN's `athlete_id` follows a player from school to school: 12,173 men and 6,684 women have lines for more than one team across seasons, and 33 men and 70 women for two teams in one season. A player's season is a player-team row; their career sums the rows once.
- **Names and keys differ by family**: stats.ncaa.org's play by play and player box name players as `AJ.DYBANTSA` (`player`) and give a `clean_name`; its schedule names teams in the NCAA's short form (`Southern Miss.`, `A&M-Corpus Christi`) with no id, so its games join to the team box by `contest_id` and to ESPN through `espn_game_id` (2023 on for men, 2024 on for women) or the schedule crosswalk.
- **Conferences change by season.** The groups file has 31 to 33 conferences a season, and teams changing conference each season: in the window men 10 in 2022, 21 in 2023, 16 in 2024, 21 in 2025, 5 in 2026 and 27 in 2027 (the women's the same), and 47 in 2014 before it. It records renames as one lineage (American, ASUN, CAA) and divisions by season (Atlantic 10 East and West 2002–2005, MAC East and West 2002–2020, Big South North and South 2013–2022, and others). ESPN labels the MAAC "Metro" in every season, as the groups' notes say. Division I grew from 327 to 365 men's teams (314 to 363 women's), 0 to 13 joining a season.
- **Neutral sites are flagged in every season of the window** (from 2008 for the men and 2009 for the women). 22 % of 2026's men's games are neutral (1,418 of 6,318): early-season events, conference and postseason tournaments. The NCAA's policies define home, away and neutral by where a team plays its home games (a team's second home arena is home; a conference tournament on a member's floor is neutral for all); whether ESPN's flag follows that rule was not checked.
- **ESPN's shot coordinates are mostly missing before 2026**: located on 6–12 % of men's and 4–5 % of women's shots in 2022–2024 and 24–26 % in 2025, every shot from 2026. The commonest located points are the two baskets' own points (41.75, 0 and −41.75, 0: 136,768 and 117,426 men's shots in 2026). stats.ncaa.org's shots have a location on every row.
- **ESPN's shot file holds made free throws and no misses**: 253,589 `MadeFreeThrow` rows among 991,836 men's shots in 2026. Leave them out to count field-goal attempts (then 98.9–99.3 % of team-games equal stats.ncaa.org's).
- **ESPN's 2026 play by play is a richer feed**: 2,915,731 men's plays for 6,275 games (465 a game) against 2,189,782 for 6,135 in 2025 (357) and 319 a game in 2022. Counts of plays by type are not comparable across the change, and a 2022 play costs 147 bytes in DuckDB against 80 in 2026.
- **Game length is 40 minutes in both** (team minutes 200 in regulation; stats.ncaa.org's team box averages 39.3 to 39.7 minutes a team-game and its players 197 to 201). Overtime is five minutes: 2026 had men's games to period 5 (three overtimes, 9 games) and women's to period 8 (four overtimes, 1 game).
- **stats.ncaa.org's team box carries the estimated possessions and ratings** (`o_poss`, `ortg`) and the opponent's counts (`d_*`); its rates are published values. Rates stay stored as their parts (policy 3) and are recomputed.
- **Ids are integers in ESPN's files and text in stats.ncaa.org's** (`contest_id`, `player_id`, `espn_game_id` as VARCHAR). Cast once, in the builders; the loader keeps each as published (policy 1). **Some ESPN files publish an id as text in one season** (the first load, T1302.3), so the loader widens the column to VARCHAR for every season: the men's `athlete_id` in the 2025-26 rosters, the women's `game_id` in the 2021-22 game rosters and officials, and `group_id` in the 2021-22 standings. A join on them casts.
- **The stats.ncaa.org team crosswalk publishes `season` as text, `2025-26`** (`ncaa_espn_team_crosswalk`, `ncaa_espn_school_seasons`). The loader reads it as the final year, `2026`, as every other file's season (`seasonSpan` in `sources.ts`).
- **The ratings and player-value files cover every team ESPN knows**: `mbb_ratings` has 727 teams in 2026, Division I and not. Rank them among the season's Division I teams.
- **stats.ncaa.org's shot file has no shot type, no assist, and no period or clock before 2026-27** (T1302.6), and its zones are radial (`paint` is 4 to 15 feet from the basket, not the lane). A shooter with no id (a school outside Division I) is a code, `AuPace`, two letters of the first name and the last name; a team with no id is named only. Read the team from the game's other side and the shooter from the code within the team.
- **stats.ncaa.org's lineup file loses time** (T1302.6): a team-game's stints add up to the game's minutes on 55 to 60 % of team-games, the rest 1 to 8 minutes short. Its stints file covers about 32 of 40 minutes a game. Neither is a source of minutes to rely on alone; the box score's minutes are ESPN's.
- **Charted corner threes scatter inside the corner line** (T1302.6): 1.0 to 1.7 % of them sit between 21.0 and 21.65 feet across, the line being 21 feet 7⅞ inches. Read a shot's value from `point_value`, never from its coordinates against the line.

**Before 2021-22, outside the window** (one line each, should it ever widen): the women played halves until 2014-15. The 2018 NIT was played in quarters (31 games with 4 periods). Neutral sites are unflagged before 2007-08 and 2008-09. 2019-20 has no postseason and 2020-21 is short (4,285 and 3,823 games). The three-point line moved in 2008-09 and 2019-20 for the men, and in 2011-12 and 2021-22 for the women, so the window has one line for both: 22 feet 1¾ inches. ESPN's women's box scores are a sample before 2016-17, often zeros. stats.ncaa.org's 2009-10 files are broken (a quarter of the player lines, no women's ids). The women's schedule crosswalk starts in 2020-21. Old stats.ncaa.org team ids differ (51431 against 609609 for one school).

## Qualifying minimums

The NCAA's Statistics Policies and Guidelines ("Ranking Minimums", read on 2026-11-18): "To be ranked, a student-athlete must appear in 75 percent of the team's games." The minimums for each category "shall be determined by the national office … and shall be reviewed annually". They are published with the rankings on stats.ncaa.org, which refused a plain client, and the record books print each season's champion without the category's minimum. National per-game rankings count "the number of games an individual actually plays", and a player in for one play has a game. A team must play the minimum number of games set for sports sponsorship to be ranked at all; the men's record book gives 13 games as the minimum for record-book inclusion in 2021, "the minimum required for NCAA championship consideration".

So the games rule can be the NCAA's own: 75 % of the team's games. The category minimums are not printed for a season (the policies leave them to the national office, `ForSIDs_Policies.txt` lines 825-828; the men's book prints "Min. 3 assists per game" on the season assist-to-turnover record, line 231, and per-game career minimums, lines 94, 137 and 211; the women's book none). Measured on the record books' champions (T1302.5), the NCAA asks a count per game played: 5 field goals, 2.5 free throws, 3 assists, and threes 2.5 for the men and 2.0 for the women, which the app adopts as the NCAA's minimums as its champions show them (D43, added 2026-11-19). Reading stats.ncaa.org's footnotes in a browser would confirm them.
