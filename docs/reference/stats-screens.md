# Stats screens: reference

What each screen shows and where its numbers come from, in all four leagues: the NBA, the WNBA, NCAA Men and NCAA Women. Screens are added here as they are built; the data they read is in [stats-database.md](stats-database.md).

**Every league (Plans T1301.9 and T1302.9).** Every screen reads the league on screen from the registry (`src/shared/leagues.ts`), never a league id: season labels (`2025-26` and `2026`), per-minute modes (per 36 and per 48, or per 36 and per 40), season types (no play-in in the WNBA, the Commissioner's Cup final), periods (the WNBA's halves to 2005), the playoff format, the models' constants and each source's first season. Copy that names a source or a count names the league's own: "As the WNBA counts them", "From the WNBA's shot chart file, 1997 on, located from 2011", "The WNBA's file lists no officials for this game." Screen lines are written once with `{league}` and `{shotsFrom}` and resolved by `inLeague` (`lib/stats/league.ts`); the catalogue's words, written for the NBA, read the league's own through the registry's `catalogueWords`, applied in `metricIn` to the metrics the league has. **What a league does not have is never offered:** a metric it lacks is left out of the column picker, every family preset and default, Explore's presets and Compare's default rows, and a dashboard tab whose every metric it lacks is not shown (the WNBA's Tracking, Play types and Matchups: no release publishes them); the Glossary lists those metrics with the reason. A metric that exists in the league but not in a season reads its reason as before. The WNBA's leaderboards qualify by the NBA's minimums scaled to its season (D37), and every line that states a minimum says so. **The college leagues** (T1302, from 2021-22, D39): while a league is off, each screen reads "No NCAA men's stats yet" or "No NCAA women's stats yet" with the button to the Data panel; once loaded, every screen reads it (T1302.9). A player linked to the paired league (NCAA Men with the NBA, NCAA Women with the WNBA, D46 to D48) has the career strip on the Overview, the hint above the name, the league menu's move to their page and the search's rows ("College and pro career", "League menu", "Rail"). Their season types are the events (D45): "NCAA tournament" (the `playoffs` type under the league's name), the NIT, CBI, CIT, The Basketball Classic and the College Basketball Crown for the men, the WNIT, WBI and WBIT for the women, all counted together as "Every game", which a scope starts on, in November too while the current season has only its regular season so far (T1304.3): the current season's numbers are then its regular season's, and the past seasons of a career, a trend or a chart count every game, as the career strip and the standings do. The scope bar offers "Every game" and "Regular season" in such a season; the team header's conference place shows on "Every game", the type the college standings count; the Lineups tab's published units ask for one type, as they do on "Every game" in any season. Their per-game leaders qualify on 75 % of the team's games, rounded up (D43): "Per game: 75 % of the team's games to qualify, the league's own rule"; the shooting leaders keep the NBA's makes, scaled, and the lines say so.

## Shell

- **Nav:** the mark, Metis, its version and the league menu (below), then Leaderboards, Players, Teams, Standings, Explore, Games, Glossary. Players and Teams open a dashboard, with the leaderboards a click on from it. Until the tables are built, every screen says what the load is doing: loading, paused, out of disk space, or stopped until the next refresh.
- **Scope** (below): shared by every screen of a league, and kept for the session.
- **A refresh that rebuilds the tables** (`status.builtAt` changes) re-reads the seasons and runs every query on screen again. A status line ticking through a load does not.
- **Status line** (`stats.status()`), read again as a load goes, at most once a second:

| It says                                               | When                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No stats yet                                          | Nothing is loaded, and nothing is running                                       |
| No WNBA stats yet                                     | The league is off, with no file; not an error                                   |
| WNBA files loaded · no tables yet                     | Loaded, its tables not built yet (the registry's `derived`, or a build to come) |
| Loading the stats · 412 of 3,070 files                | The first load, before the tables are built                                     |
| Data through Nov 4, 2026 · updating 71 of 3,070 files | A load or a refresh is running                                                  |
| Data through Nov 4, 2026 · updated 2 h ago            | Nothing is running, in the league's season months                               |
| 2025-26 season complete · data through Jun 18, 2026   | Between seasons, with the data through the season just played (below)           |
| Data through Nov 4, 2026 · paused, or Loading paused  | The owner paused                                                                |
| Not enough disk space                                 | The first load could not start                                                  |
| Stats unavailable                                     | The stats database cannot be read                                               |

**The date** is the last day with a played game in the league's `game` table (`status.through`, in `service.ts`), since that is what every screen can show; a game is played there once the league's schedule has its score. It is not a source's box scores, which can run a day ahead: on Nov 21, 2026, ESPN's file had that day's six NBA games while the NBA's schedule had no score for them yet, and the line read Nov 21 while every screen stopped on Nov 20. Before the tables are built there is no date, and the line says what the load is doing. Games' "Not played" and "Scheduled", the team schedule's next game, the season complete line, Trends' seasons and the Favourites page's subtitle and section notes read the same date.

**Between seasons** (`freshness`, `seasonComplete` in `lib/stats/scope.ts`), one rule for every league: outside the registry's `seasonMonths` (the NBA's October to June, the WNBA's May to October), and when the data runs through the season whose months just ended, the line says that season is complete and the date its data runs through, and nothing about when it was updated, since a date weeks old is not stale then. A refresh or a pause still adds "· updating…" or "· paused". Data that stops short of the season just played reads as in season, as it is not complete. The NBA reads "2025-26 season complete" from July to September, the WNBA "2026 season complete" from November to April.

The status line opens the Data panel.

## League menu

D35: the whole app shows one league at a time. The menu sits beside the wordmark and names the league on screen (by name, never colour: D17). It lists every league in the registry (`LEAGUES`), each with its full name and a note from its real state (`api.leagues.downloads()`, read through `useLeagueDownloads`): "No stats yet" beside a league that is off with no file, "No tables yet" beside one turned on whose tables are not built yet: downloading with nothing loaded, or loaded with its tables still to build, from the league's own status (`builtAt`, read beside the downloads since T1304.5; before, only the registry's `derived` could say so, and a league turned on read as ready at once), and nothing beside one that is ready. It is a Radix menu: Enter, Space or ↓ opens it, the arrows move, Enter picks, Esc closes, and the focus comes back to the menu's button after a switch.

**What a switch keeps.** The shell is built again for the league picked (`App` keys `StatsApp` by it), so every screen, board, search and Glossary entry reads that league: its seasons, catalogue, season labels, season types and per-minute modes, all from the registry. The sessions live above the shell (`lib/stats/session.ts`):

- **The screen is the app's.** A switch keeps it: a leaderboard stays a leaderboard, with its tab; the Glossary stays the Glossary.
- **Each league keeps its own session**, as the shell held it on `main`: the scope (season, season type, range, last N), the dashboards' player, team and tab, Games' day and game, Explore's tab and the Chart's, Compare's and Trends' state, and what opened a leaderboard last. A league not yet on screen this session starts from a fresh session and its own registry defaults (its latest season, the regular season, per game), so nothing one league lacks crosses over: per 48 in a 40-minute league, the play-in in the WNBA, an NBA season label.
- **A screen's own state** (a leaderboard's board: its tab, columns, mode and sort; the home's per-game switch; the dashboards' per-game or per-minute mode; the Glossary's filters; the standings' grouping) is kept by league while that screen stays on show (`useScreenState`), and goes when another screen opens, as it always has.

**The switch rule** (`locationAfterSwitch`): every screen keeps its place, and a screen about one subject shows the new league's own subject as it was left there, or the screen's default when there is none yet. It never carries the old league's subject over: a player, team or game id belongs to one league.

| On screen                                                     | After a switch                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Leaderboards, a leaderboard, Standings, Explore, the Glossary | The same screen and tab, in the new league's scope                                            |
| A player's dashboard                                          | The new league's player dashboard, on the player left there, else its leading scorer in scope |
| A team's dashboard                                            | The new league's team dashboard, on the team left there, else the best record in scope        |
| Games, with a game open                                       | The new league's Games, on the day or game left there, else its latest day                    |
| Compare with picked players or teams                          | The new league's Compare, with its own picks, else none                                       |
| The Data panel                                                | The Data panel, which lists every league                                                      |

Switching back gives the first league everything as it was left, the same player's dashboard included.

**The same player across a pair** (T1304.4, D47). One exception to the rule above: on a linked player's dashboard (the career strip's link, NBA with NCAA Men, WNBA with NCAA Women), with the paired league downloaded and built, choosing that league opens the same player's dashboard there, on the Overview, in their latest season in that league: the league's default scope for a player who plays there now (Cooper Flagg's NBA 2026-27), their last college season for one who has left (his NCAA Men 2024-25, on Every game), so the page opens on their games rather than on "played no games in 2026-27". The paired league's item says so in its second line, in the full name's place, so the item keeps its size: "Cooper Flagg’s page" (on hover, "Opens Cooper Flagg’s page in NCAA Men"). The move goes through `openPage` in `App.tsx`, the strip's rows' way, so it is one page for Back: Back returns to the page left, and choosing the league again repeats the move. The page offers the move only once its read of the other career is in (after its own reads, as the strip): a menu opened before that, a league outside the pair, a player with no link (LeBron James), the paired league not downloaded or still loading, or a draftee with no game there (no page to open) all switch as the table says, and the item reads as it always has. The logic is in `lib/stats/leagueSwitch.ts`.

**Back.** Every page reported for Back carries its league (`NavLocation.league`), so a switch is a page of its own: Back after a switch returns to the page before it, in the league it belongs to, and switches back to that league. Back never shows one league's player, team or game under the other's name. **Back after a move to another season or league** (T1304.5) returns to the page as it was left, its season, season type and range included: the strip's rows, the league menu's move to the same player, and the search's rows of another season or league (Kobe Bryant, 2015-16, from Cooper Flagg's page in 2026-27: Back shows Flagg in 2026-27, not in 2015-16 with no games). Each page reports its scope with it, but a change of scope alone is no new page; only a move through `openPage` that opens another season or league (`openMove` in `App.tsx`) marks the page it leaves to keep its scope (`leave` in `lib/navHistory.ts`). Any other Back leaves the scope as it is, as it always has: a player opened from the rail, the season changed on their page, then Back, shows the page before in that season. The app has no Forward.

**Remembered.** The league on screen is kept across launches in the renderer's storage (`metis.league`, beside the theme): the app opens on the league it was left on, the NBA when none is saved. The sessions are not: each league opens on its own defaults at the next launch, as scope always has.

**Saved views** are the league's own (`stats_views` is keyed by league): the Views menu lists the league on screen's, a view saved there belongs to it, and opening one opens it in that league. Nothing switches league from a view.

**A league with nothing to read.** Every screen but the Data panel shows its name and the reason the league's status carries. It never shows an error, a spinner that does not end, or a table without words.

- **Off, with no file:** the screen reads the registry's `noData` ("No WNBA stats yet: turn the WNBA on in the Data panel to download it.") and the status line reads "No WNBA stats yet". A button under the reason, `TURN_ON` in `lib/stats/league.ts` ("Turn it on in the Data panel"), opens the Data panel, where the league is turned on.
- **On and loaded, with no tables built yet:** the screen reads the registry's `notBuilt` ("No WNBA stats yet: its files are loaded, and the tables built from them come in a later version."). The status line says what the load is doing, then "WNBA files loaded · no tables yet".

The Data panel always opens, whatever the league holds, and shows every league.

## Scope bar

- **Season:** every season with a box score, newest first, written `2025-26`.
- **Season type:** only those the season has box scores for: Regular season, Play-in, Playoffs, NBA Cup final, Regular season and after (when there is an after), All-Star (the 17 games ESPN has). Preseason games have no box score anywhere, so they are not offered. Another season falls back to the regular season if it does not have the type. In the WNBA: Regular season, Playoffs, Commissioner's Cup final (2021 on, ESPN's box score alone), Regular season and after, never All-Star (its sides are not league teams, so it has no team lines). The Cup final is its own type, counted in "Regular season and after" as the NBA Cup final is, and never in "Regular season": the league's own totals leave it out of the regular season (T1301.5).
- **Range:** Whole season; Dates, two pickers held inside the season, starting on its first and last games; or Last games, the last 5, 10, 15, 20 or 30, of the player's own games or their team's. Moving to another season drops a date range, since its dates were the old season's.

## Choosing a team

Plan T1705: one control wherever a team is picked from a list (`components/stats/TeamChooser.tsx`, `lib/stats/teamSearch.ts`), since a college league has 365. It is the team page's code between the arrows, the team filter of the Players board, the rail and Explore's chart ("All teams" first), Compare's team slots and Trends' team box. No plain select of teams is left; the Filters control's Team attribute, which takes several codes, keeps its own searchable values.

- **The list.** The same teams in the same order as the select had, opened on the team chosen now, the search at its top. A row reads the code, the name, the favourite's star and, quiet, the conference where the list carries it.
- **Typing** narrows by the name (which holds the city or school) and the code; accents, case and punctuation are ignored ("montreal", "st johns", "pine bluff"). What starts with the text, in the code, the name or a word of it, comes first, then what only holds it, each in the list's own order: "mont" lists Montana, Montana State, Belmont, Vermont. Nothing found reads "No team matches".
- **Keys.** A click, ↓, ↑ or a letter opens it, the letter starting the search. ↓ ↑ and Page Up and Down move, Enter takes, Escape and Tab close and leave the choice. Enter rests on the team of the very code typed ("unc" on North Carolina, though UNC Asheville is listed before it), else on the first match. Its keys never reach the rail it sits in, which moves on the same arrows.
- **What is stored does not change:** ids, and Explore's players by code, as saved views and the session hold them. A stored team the season lacks is named by its own code, where the select fell back to "All teams" over a filter still in force.

## Leaderboards (home)

Eight boards, the top six of each: points, rebounds, assists, threes made, steals, blocks, true shooting, and team net rating. Per game or totals, per game first. Per game, a count needs 70 % of the most games anyone played in scope: the NBA's own rule is 58 of 82, and reading it from the result holds for a date range too. The line under the title states it for the season on screen: "Per game: 58 games of 82 to qualify", 35 of 50 in 1998-99, and in the WNBA "Per game: 31 games of 44 to qualify, the NBA's minimum scaled, until the WNBA's own are read" (24 of 34 from 2003 to 2019). A rate needs its own sample. "All →" opens the full leaderboard sorted by that metric, in the same mode. A row opens that player's or team's dashboard. Saved views are listed above the boards.

## Players and Teams

A table by category (D52, `stat-categories.md`; Plan T1702.3), over a position group (All players, Guards, Forwards, Centers) or, for teams, a side.

- **Category tabs:** Overview, then each category the league has for the kind of row (`boardCategories` in `lib/stats/categoryViews.ts`): the NBA players' twelve (Offense, Defense, Scoring, Shooting, Playmaking, Rebounding, Fouls, Hustle and movement, Play types, Impact, Results and strength, Playing time), ten in the WNBA and the colleges, which have no hustle or play types; teams add Predictive and have no Playing time. They fit one line at 1280 px (the NBA players' last tab ends at 1,124 px) and wrap to a second on a narrower window, never hidden. The title names what is on screen: "2026-27 guard shooting leaderboard", "2026-27 player defense leaderboard", and for teams the side: "2026-27 team shooting leaderboard · opponents". Overview is the tab's own columns in named groups (`src/renderer/src/lib/stats/leaderboard.ts`), as every board before categories.
- **Position or side:** players' positions (All players, Guards, Forwards, Centers) keep the columns. A team's specific category (Shooting, Scoring…) has a Team side and an Opponents side. In the categories about what a side does with the ball (Scoring, Shooting, Playmaking) a metric shows on the side of its end of the floor: the team's shots blocked (`opp_blk`) and turnovers lost to steals (`opp_stl`) read on the Team side beside its field goals and turnovers, and the opponents' turnovers and FG% (`opp_tov`, `opp_fg_pct`) on the Opponents side; in the others (Rebounding, Fouls, Hustle and movement and the rest) the side is whose box score it is (`teamSide`); Overview keeps its ends (Offense, Defense) with their default columns; Offense and Defense need no switch, since each holds its end whoever's columns hold it: Defense has the team's steals and the opponents' scoring together. A team's tabs are the same on either side, and a side with nothing in a category reads "No opponents' metrics in Hustle and movement."
- **Presets** as chips under them (`categoryViews`): a specific category's Key stats (its core metrics, then those read from games, twelve at most, in sub-group order), then each of its sub-groups; every view holds at most 40 metrics, the query's limit, which a test holds in all four leagues. Offense and Defense open on chosen lists (`UMBRELLA_KEY_STATS` in `lib/stats/categoryViews.ts`, the manager's review of T1702.3), a metric the league lacks skipped and the next taken, twelve at most: for teams the rating and the four factors lead at both ends (ORtg, PTS, eFG%, TS%, TOV%, ORB%, FT rate, then FG%, 3PM, 3P%, AST and the adjusted offence where the league has it; DRtg, Opp PTS, Opp eFG%, Opp TS%, Opp TOV%, DRB%, Opp FT rate, then Opp FG%, Opp 3P%, STL, BLK and the adjusted defence); for players PTS, TS%, eFG%, FG%, 3PM, 3P%, FT%, AST, AST%, TOV%, USG%, then the offensive on-court rating or model, and STL, BLK, STL%, BLK%, DREB, DRB%, rim FG% allowed, deflections, contested shots, then the defensive on-court rating and models; then their headings (Scoring, Shooting, Playmaking, Turnovers…); a heading over 40 metrics opens a second line of the specific sub-groups of that end ("Shooting › Field goals, Twos, Threes…"). A chip fills the board with its metrics and keeps the sort if its column stays, else sorts by the first, best first. "Your columns" marks a board whose columns were changed from its preset. The column groups over the table name each column's sub-group in the category (Threes, Efficiency), Overview's its named groups.
- **Saved views** keep the category and preset (`LeaderboardBoard.category`, `view`); a view saved before them opens on Overview with its own columns, sort, mode and position exactly as saved (checked with three views saved by the 1.3.0 build and reopened).

- **Stability mark** (Plan T1006): three bars beside every metric's column head, filled three for high, two for medium, one for low and none when not measured, from the year-to-year correlation (`docs/reference/stats-database.md`, "Metric stability"). The head's card, and the mark's own hover, read it in short lines: "Year to year 0.70 on 3,931 pairs", "Split half 0.81 on 5,322 seasons", "Prior 187 field-goal attempts", "Measured 1993-94 to 2025-26"; a metric with no measure says why ("Not measured: Published without games played."), and before the first measure "Not measured yet: a refresh measures it.". The mark is on the leaderboards, the dashboards' stat lines, the career, season-by-season, roster, split and game-log tables, the Standings' own columns (PYTH W, SRS, SOS, ELO and the forecast's, which read "Not measured: A forecast read at a season's end is the outcome"), Compare and the column picker. It rides with the catalogue, so it costs no query; it is hidden from screen readers, which would read it into every head's name, and the card says the same in words.
- **Filters:** the mode, the moments of the game, Qualified only, Shrunk rates, team (players), search by name, which finds a name without its accents. Search filters the engine's rows, so percentiles stay among the whole position.
- **Filters** (T1701.2, D51): a button in the toolbar, beside Columns (`components/stats/FiltersControl.tsx`, `lib/stats/filters.ts`). It opens the conditions, each editable, over every attribute of who the rows are (Personal, Body, Draft, Career, Season, Combine; for teams Team and Arena) and every metric the tab offers, listed as the column picker lists them, under each category and sub-group they sit in (`pickerGroups`, D52), all searchable. Where the registry holds the same kind of fact from two sources or times, each says whose listing it is under its label (`AttributeDef.byline`): "Listed country, the league's listing, from its bio" beside "Birth country, where born, from ESPN"; "Height, as last published", "Height that season, as listed for the season" and "Height without shoes, measured at the draft combine"; the city and the arena's city. An attribute the league does not have is listed, greyed, with the registry's reason under its label ("No college file publishes it."). A number takes at least, at most or between; a date the same; text any of or none of, picked from the values the league holds in the seasons in scope, searchable and each with how many rows hold it (the 3,000 most held), or contains, typed; an enumeration (a college class) its values as toggles; a flag yes or no; anything "is known" or "is not known". A percentage is typed in percent; a metric is compared in the board's mode, and its chip says which ("Points at least 15 (per game)"). The active conditions are chips under the toggles, each with a button to remove it; one not finished is dashed and not asked for. Every condition holds at once.
  - **What the rows are:** the engine's rows that pass, numbered 1 to n. Each keeps the shading of the whole board (`rankFiltered`). In the NBA and the WNBA the board holds every row and ranks on the client, so it still reads its unfiltered rows and a filtered row takes that board's percentile, the same with or without the filter (driven: Luka Dončić, Nikola Jokić and Joel Embiid, every cell's colour equal with "Birth country is none of USA" and without). In the colleges the engine ranks for the board (below), so a filtered row reads the engine's figure, inside the board's first 1,000 or past them: in NCAA Men 2026-27, "Class is any of Fr." lists 644 rows; Jordan Smith Jr. reads the 82nd percentile in points, Jacob Lanier, 2,617th on the whole board, the 21st. Favourites, the team, search and "Qualified only" narrow the filtered rows as they narrow the board.
  - **Left out:** a row with no value for a condition, and passing every condition it has a value for, is left out and counted in the footer: "9 players in scope left out: no value for Birth country." The count is over every row in scope, qualified or not. A filter on experience adds that experience is the season's roster's: a player it does not list has none (LeBron James has no 2025-26 roster line). A condition no row can answer, an attribute outside its seasons, says why in the footer instead ("Arena capacity: published from 2001-02 to 2018-19.").

A board's percentiles and ranks are among the rows the tab qualifies, per position: on the client where the board holds every row (`rankWithinTab`, the NBA and the WNBA), from the engine where it does not (`StatsQuery.rankAmong`, the colleges' 5,000 players against a fetch of 1,000). Before the fix a college board ranked among its first 1,000 alone: NCAA Men 2026-27 by points a game, row 500 read the 46th percentile and row 1,000 the 0th; they now read the 82nd and the 85th, the place among every qualified guard. Ranks were right throughout.
  - **The values a list offers** are read through the worker (`attributeValues`, `src/main/stats/attributeReads.ts`): a player's attribute over the players with a counted game in the seasons, one that changes by season over their player-seasons, a team's over its team-seasons (Division I in the colleges), most held first. Read once a league, attribute and seasons and kept for the session. Measured on the four-league copy, 2026-11-23, five reads each with other work on the machine: 48 to 97 ms in the median (NBA birth countries of 2026-27, 42 values, 80 ms; every NBA school 1993-94 to 2026-27, 527, 69 ms; NCAA Men high schools 2021-22 to 2026-27, the 3,000 most held, 97 ms).
  - **Kept** with the board: across its tabs, and in a saved view (`LeaderboardBoard.filters`, as the engine reads them, a percentage as a fraction). A view saved before filters opens with none.
- **Shrunk rates** (Plan T1006): each rate with a measured prior pulled toward the league's rate in the same scope by it, so a small sample reads close to the league and a large one close to its own. Shrunk columns carry an S on the head, and a note under the table says what was done and names any rate with no prior; counts and rates without one read unchanged. A shrunk column ranks and shades every row with a sample, the ones below the qualifier too, which is what shrinking is for: turning it on turns Qualified only off, so those rows show, still marked NQ, and turning it off turns Qualified only back on. The value's hover adds its raw figure ("Raw 70.0% on 225 field-goal attempts, shrunk toward the league"). On the 2025-26 board sorted by TS%, Goga Bitadze (972 minutes) ranks 14th shrunk, 64.6 % from a raw 70.0 % on 225 attempts; unshrunk he is hidden below the qualifier.
- **Moments of the game:** Whole game, or one split counted from the play by play (Plan T902): a quarter, a half, overtime, clutch, ahead, tied, behind, within 5, ahead or behind by 10+, the last 2 minutes of a period, the last 2 minutes or the last minute of the game, garbage time or the rest. It sits on the board, not on the scope bar: the bar is shared by every screen, and a split there would change every dashboard and the home boards at once. The rule is the first note under the table ("Clutch: The last 5 minutes of the 4th quarter or overtime, the score within 5. Counted from the NBA's play by play."); in a WNBA season of halves (to 2005) a second note says the 4th quarter's rules read the second half. Only the moments in the split count; a player's games are the games they had any of them in. A metric the plays cannot rebuild (tracking, hustle, play types, the NBA's dashboards, plus-minus, paint points) reads blank with "Not counted from the play by play, so it has no split." on hover, and a season before 1996-97 "Splits are counted from the play by play, from 1996-97.". The qualifying sample scales to the split's share of the time as well as to the games: clutch is about 3.7 of a team's 48 minutes a game in 2025-26, so it needs about 75 minutes where the whole game needs 1,000. A saved view keeps its moment.
- **Columns:** the column picker lists every metric the league has for the kind of row by category and sub-group (`pickerGroups`), a metric under each category it sits in; it opens on the board's category, "Every category" lists them all, and a team's lists both sides. Search, Reset (to the preset's columns, or the tab's on Overview), at most 40. Before Stage 17 the presets were the families, "Columns: this tab" or one family’s metrics (Traditional, Shooting, League indexes (Plan T1006, in the catalogue's order: TS+, eFG+, FG%+, 2P%+, 3P%+, FT%+, then the rate indexes, 100 the league in the same scope), Possessions, Scoring, Composite, Box-score ratings, Tracking, Hustle, Play types, Matchups, Impact (SportsDataverse), Availability; the NBA’s advanced, misc, scoring, usage and defense dashboards sit in the first five; and for teams Ratings, twelve columns in its own order, Plans T1001, T1002 and T1004: MOV, SRS, Elo, adjusted net rating, Pythagorean %, Pythagorean wins, wins over Pythagorean, SOS, SOS left, adjusted offensive and defensive ratings, wins Elo expected; Pythagorean losses, games left, wins over Elo and the adjusted home court are in the picker); the column picker adds or removes any metric (at most 40); Reset.
- **Sort:** a column head (best first, then the other way), or the name. The engine sorts; the table shows 50 rows at a time.
- **Views:** a named board saved in `metis.db` (`stats_views`): entity, tab, columns, sort, mode, qualified only, shrunk rates, team, filters. The season and dates stay the screen's. Saving under an existing name, in any case, replaces it.
- **CSV:** every row the filters keep, not only the 50 shown: rank, name, team, position and id, games, then each column unformatted, percentages in percent. Saved where the dialog says (Downloads by default), UTF-8 with a byte-order mark so Excel reads accents.
- **Notes** under the table state what the answer depends on: a stat's first season, estimated possessions, a season with no All-Star Game.
- **Games out** (Availability, Plan T1206): a player listed Out of every game of the season or of the playoff run in scope has no box-score line and is still listed, from the injury reports, with G 0, every box-score column blank and NQ on hover "No games played in range". Name, team and position are the report's and the roster's; one the sources do not give stays blank. Such a row never qualifies, ranks or shades, whatever the column. Sorted by a games-out count, "Qualified only" keeps every row with games out, those below the minutes or with no line marked NQ: games out have no minimum to reach, and hiding them would hide the players most out. Sorted by anything else, those rows are hidden like any NQ row, and a board that reads no games-out column lists exactly whom it listed before. Games out are whole-season counts, so a date range, the last games, a split or any filter on games reads the column's reason and adds nobody. In 2025-26, 11 players with no line in the regular season (Tyrese Haliburton, out of all 82 games, among them) and 16 in the playoffs.

- **The play-derived counts** (the Play by play tab until T1702.4, now by category; the game flow in Results and strength): the player's for the team, ranked among the teams, with a Game flow section first: lead changes a game, the largest lead and the largest deficit, time leading, the best run for and against, the longest stretches without a point and without a field goal; team turnovers (no player charged) beside the kinds; the opponents' live-ball turnovers, shooting fouls and and-ones. - **Players:** everyone who played for the team in scope, most minutes first: a traded player's games here, not their season.
- **Splits:** the team's games split as the player's are (home and road, wins and losses, before and after the All-Star Game, rest, the opponent's conference and division), then the same play splits, then by month and by opponent: points for and against, offensive, defensive and net rating, pace, the four factors. Under a play split, possessions are counted from the plays (SportsDataverse's derived possessions), not estimated, and the note says so. Added with Plan T902: the team dashboard had no Splits tab before.
- **Lineups:** the team's units for the season in scope, as the NBA publishes them (the colleges' are built: the heading says from stats.ncaa.org's play by play or lineup file): of five, four, three or two players (a **Players** control, five to start, shown once the file holds the smaller groups), one dashboard's columns at a time: Base, Advanced, Four factors, Misc, Scoring, Opponent. Each row has the players (a name opens that player), games and minutes together. The mode applies to the counts: totals, per game, per 36 and per 48 minutes, per 100 possessions (the advanced file's possessions; blank with its reason where the unit is not in that file). Ratings and shares read as published. Click a head to sort, either way, blanks last. The minimum-minutes control starts at 25, and the list shows 15 with "Show all". A unit a file does not list reads — with its reason on hover. Each file lists at most 2,000 units of a size and season type with the most minutes (every regular season, and the playoffs' threes and fours), and when a list reached it the screen says where that floor is. Groups of fewer than five count whatever the rest of the floor was, so they overlap, and the screen says so. A unit is a whole season: a date range or the last games reads "Published for whole seasons only.", seasons before 2007-08 "Recorded from 2007-08.", and Regular season and after asks for one of the two. A **Source** control above it chooses **Published** (the NBA's, as above) or **From possessions**: Metis's own count from the play by play, every group in every season from 1996-97, of five, four, three or two players, with games, minutes, possessions, plus-minus, ratings, pace and four factors, a minimum-possessions control (50 to start), sorting either way and 15 rows before "Show all". It answers the scope's season types, a date range and the team's last games, and says it is derived, not official.
- **On/off** (last): each player who played for the team in scope, most minutes first, with their minutes and possessions on the floor, plus-minus, ratings on, net off and on less off; a row opens the player. Derived, not official, and off the floor is the rest of the player's own games.
- **Schedule:** every game in the scope's season type, with the result and the record after it. A game past its day with no score reads "Not played", like the one cancelled in 2013. A row opens the game.

## Games

A day's games on a date rail, in place of the template's weeks. Opens on the latest day a game that counts was played, and a game opened from a log or a schedule opens on its day.

- **Rail:** the season, the day (a list of days with a game, and the day before or after), then that day's games: away over home, the winner in bold, its type if not the regular season, overtime, "Not played".
- **Header:** each team's name, score, its record and last five results before this game in the same kind of game, then the status (Final, Final/OT, Not played, Scheduled), the date, ESPN's note ("NBA Finals - Game 5"), the arena and the crowd. A team opens its dashboard.
- **Officials:** under the header, "Officials: …" from the league's file, or "The NBA's file lists no officials for this game." (before 2003-04 most games; the WNBA's from 2004, "The WNBA's file lists no officials for this game."). A name opens that official's seasons.
- **Score by period:** when ESPN has it and it adds up to the final score: through 2000-01 and from 2019-20. A period is named by its season's format (`periodsIn`): the WNBA's two halves to 2005 read 1, 2, OT.
- **Elo** (Plan T1002): a small table, away then home, of both ratings before the game, after it once played, and the win probability before it, with a line for the home court ("Home court: 80 Elo points.", or "Neutral site: no home court.") and the expected margin ("Expected: GSW by 3.2."). A game still to come in the season in progress reads the latest ratings, so its probability moves as games are played. In 1993-94 and 1994-95 it reads "Elo is read from 1995-96: every team starts at 1500 in 1993-94, so 1993-94 and 1994-95 settle the ratings." and shows no ratings; in the WNBA, 1997 to 2000 ("Elo is read from 2001: every team starts at 1500 in 1997, so 1997 to 2000 settle the ratings."). All-Star and preseason games have none.
- **Game flow:** from 1996-97, between the score by period and the box scores: the home side's margin over the game clock as a step line, shaded on the side ahead, period and overtime breaks dashed, a dot at each lead change. Under it, the lead changes, the times tied and the time level, then each side's largest lead, time leading, best run, and longest stretches without a point and without a field goal, with the rules in a line. Before 1996-97 it reads "Recorded from 1996-97."; a game the feed lacks, "No play by play for this game.". It reads one game's scoring plays through its own call (`gameFlow`) and works the numbers out by the rules the tables use.
- **Box score:** one table a team, starters then bench, with minutes as a clock and made and attempted together. A player opens their dashboard. The totals are the team's own box score, and points the final score. With nobody flagged as starting, one list says who started is not listed. When the players' lines do not add up to the final score, the screen says so: 36 of 83,516 team-games when measured (Plan T605), nearly all in 1994-95.
- **The teams:** twelve lines side by side, the better side in bold, with the reason where a stat did not exist yet.
- **No box score:** preseason games have none in either source, and some old games and All-Star games have only a score, so the screen says that. It never fabricates a line.
- **Who guarded whom:** under the box scores, from 2017-18, the game's pairs from the NBA's game matchups, one table for each side on defense, away first: the defender, the player guarded with the position the file lists for them, the time as a clock, partial possessions, and what the player on offense did while assigned (points, field goals, threes, free throws, assists, turnovers, blocks). Longest first, 15 a side and "Show every pair". Either name opens that player. Before 2017-18 it reads "Tracked from 2017-18."; a played game the file lacks reads "The NBA’s file has no matchups for this game." (8 of 1,230 in 2017-18, none from 2022-23).

The engine reads one game by its id. A date and teams would not do: All-Star round robins play more than once a day.

### Officials

"Officials of 2025-26" under the rail's day count switches the page from the game to the season's officials, and back. The rail stays, so a game is one click away.

- **A season's officials:** every official the NBA's file lists for the season and the season type chosen beside the heading (regular season, play-in, playoffs, Cup final, or all of them), in order of games worked, then name: no column orders the list, so nothing reads as a ranking. The league's line comes first, over every game of the same season and types. A name opens that official's seasons.
- **An official's seasons:** newest first, each season's line with the league's line for the same season and types just below it, so the reader sees the ordinary spread. "The game" or "Officials" returns to where they came from.
- **The columns:** games worked with both box scores; personal fouls and free-throw attempts per game, both teams together; fouls and free-throw attempts per game by the home side and by the away side; points per game, both teams; and the share of the games the home side won. Each is a sum over the games divided when read (`lib/stats/officials.ts`), from the box scores (`team_game`) and the game's final score.
- **Copy:** the notes under the table say the numbers describe the games an official worked, as the box scores record them, not who made each call; that every official of a crew counts the same game; and that a line of fewer than 20 games is marked "N games: few", since over a handful of games a rate moves a long way. Nothing calls an official best, worst, strict or lenient, or speaks of favouring a side.
- **Coverage:** the line under the heading says how many played games have a crew listed and the crews' sizes ("1,227 of 1,230 games have a crew listed; crews of 3 on 1,227."). Before 2003-04 the file has a crew for 0 to 8.6 % of a season's games, so the officials are still listed with their games but every rate reads — with "Crews are listed for 17 of 1,189 games in 1999-00; rates start in 2003-04.", in the season list and on each such line of an official's seasons.

Read by two calls beside the engine, `stats.officialsSeason` and `stats.officialCareer` (`src/main/stats/officialsReads.ts`), and the game's pairs by `stats.gameMatchups` (`matchupReads.ts`). On the full database, inside the worker: a game's pairs 47 ms, a player's pairs over the last 10 games 39 ms, a season's officials 188 ms, an official's seasons 391 ms.

## Explore

Chart, Compare and Trends as three tabs over the shared scope bar. Explore opens on Chart; the tab and each tab's state are kept for the session, so leaving for a dashboard and coming back finds them as they were. Back returns to the page before, not to the previous tab. Each tab is its own screen (`StatsChart`, `StatsCompare`, `StatsTrends`) with its state in `lib/stats/{chart,compare,trends}.ts`. Other screens reach Explore through two entries: Compare with a list of players or teams, and Trends on one player or team, optionally on a metric. Trends with nobody picked shows the player or team a dashboard showed last.

The scope bar has no mode, so each tab carries the dashboards' own control (Per game, Totals, Per 36, Per 48, Per 100), kept per tab. A rate is the engine's ratio of sums and reads the same in every mode; Trends shows the control only for a total the mode rescales. The scenario steps for all three tabs are in `scripts/stage8-steps.json`.

### Chart

Any metric against another, for the players at a position (All, G, F, C) or the 30 teams, in the scope bar's scope. One engine query reads the axes, size, colour and the qualifier for every row; nothing new is stored.

- **Presets:** players, usage against true shooting, three-point attempts against percentage, assist percentage against turnover percentage, and drives against points per drive (tracking, from 2013-14; not offered in the WNBA, which has no tracking); teams, offensive against defensive rating, pace against net rating, three-point rate against true shooting. Any catalogue metric can go on either axis, and the axes swap.
- **Qualified:** the leaderboards' rule. A player needs enough minutes for the games in scope, and a row needs enough of each axis's own sample; an axis with the NBA's minimum (D33) needs only that. "Qualified only" can be turned off, as on the leaderboards, and unqualified rows are greyed with NQ. Percentiles are among the qualified of the same position.
- **Size and colour:** size by a metric's percentile; colour by a metric's percentile, or by a category: the roster's position for players (G, F, C, grey when the roster gives none), the conference for teams. Only metrics with a better side can size or colour.
- **The plot:** a lower-is-better axis runs reversed, so up and right is better on both, and the corners say so in words. The dashed lines are the qualified means, and r is worked with both axes oriented the same way. The searched and the picked are labelled, then the eight players farthest from the crowd, or every team.
- **Clicks:** a dot or a table row opens the dashboard, and Back returns to the chart as it was. Shift-click picks up to three, and "Compare these" opens them in Compare.
- **Too few qualifiers:** under 10 dots the chart says how many qualify, in what scope, and why: "4 centers qualify on both axes in the 2025-26 play-in. A scatter needs at least 10: …". It offers the whole season when a range is set, and "Show all 14 centers" when more have values.
- **No values:** an axis the scope cannot answer shows the engine's reason instead of an empty plot: "Drives: Published for whole seasons only." under a date range or last games, with a button back to the whole season, and "Drives: Recorded from 2013-14." before then.
- **Filters** (T1701.3, D51): the leaderboards' control, in the Filter section beside Favourites, its panel opening to the right, with the same attributes, metrics, value lists and chips; the league's own attributes and the rest greyed with their reason. The engine answers which rows pass, and the chart keeps their dots and rows only: the axes, the means and every percentile stay the whole field's, so a dot never moves when a filter is set, as for the favourites. The summary says "92 shown of 1522 qualified players, passing the filters", and the rows left out for no value are counted under the chips. A filter belongs to the chart's players or teams: switching between them clears it. Driven 2026-11-23: NCAA Men 2026-27, "Hometown contains “, TX”", 92 of 1,522 qualified players, 65 with no hometown; WNBA 2026, "Age at most 25", 28 of 70.
- **Row limit:** the engine returns at most 5,000 rows. A season's players are about 600, but if a query ever stops short, the chart says how many rows it left out.

Measured on the clean Stage 7 load (`scripts/stage8-steps.json`), timed through `window.api` from the renderer, with the engine's own time in brackets: players usage and true shooting 72 ms (58), three-pointers 62 (49), points, drives, assists and true shooting per game 73 (57) and in totals 76 (59), the same under a date range 44 (29), the last 10 games 48 (36), centers in the play-in 30 (25), and teams with three metrics 22 (17). A second run read 56 to 99 ms (41 to 75).

### Compare

Three slots side by side, all players or all teams, one row per metric and one column per slot (`StatsCompare`, `lib/stats/compare.ts`).

- **Slots:** each is filled from a search over the rows in scope, which finds a name without its accents (a team by name or code), and can be removed or replaced; someone already in a slot is not offered again. Switching between Players and Teams clears the slots. On the first visit the open slots take the three leaders who qualify (points per game, or net rating). A slot's name opens that player's or team's dashboard. The slots, kind, seasons, mode and rows are kept for the session.
- **From the dashboards:** a Compare button on the player and team dashboard headers opens Compare with that player or team in the first open slot (the last slot when all three are taken; a player opened over teams clears them). Back returns to the dashboard.
- **Seasons:** the scope bar's season, or the last 3, 5 or 10 seasons up to it, or every season up to it, each offered only when it reaches further back. Over several seasons the scope bar's date range or last games, which belong to one season, are not applied: the seasons are read whole, and a note says so.
- **Mode:** the shared control above; a rate is the ratio of sums over the slot's games, never an average of per-game values.
- **Rows:** the core metrics of Traditional, Shooting and Composite for players (23 rows), of Traditional, Shooting and Possessions for teams (40). "Add a category" appends that category's Key stats (D52; "Add a family" before T1702.4), the rows grouped by each metric's primary category and sub-group ("Shooting › Threes"). The default rows read as a reader reads a player: Scoring, Shooting, Playmaking, Rebounding, Defense, Usage, Impact, then the rest (a team's results first); rows the user chose, and a saved comparison's, keep the registry's order; before, a family's columns as the leaderboards define them, and the column picker adds or removes any metric, at most 40; Reset returns to the defaults. Players always keep Minutes, which qualifies them. Rows are grouped by family.
- **Best value:** bold, read the metric's way (fewer turnovers, fouls or points allowed, a lower defensive rating), at the precision shown, so two values that read alike tie and both are bold. A metric with no better side, a tie between every value, or a row with fewer than two values marks nothing. Each slot's card says how many rows it leads of those that can be led.
- **Ranks:** for one season, the query asks for every player or team in scope, so each slot shows the percentile bar and rank of the dashboards: a player among the qualified players of their own position (`rankWithinTab`; in the colleges the engine's figures, `rankAmong`, as the boards', since a season's 1,000 fetched are not every qualified player), a team among the league. Over several seasons the query asks for the three slots alone, since a pool over a span would mix careers, and no ranks are shown. A player below the minutes asked is marked NQ and has none.
- **Blanks:** never a zero. A metric with no value in scope for any slot reads its reason across the row ("Recorded from 2013-14.", "Published for whole seasons only."). Over several seasons a slot whose own seasons all predate a metric reads blank with "Recorded from 2013-14." on hover; one whose seasons start before it shows the value over the covered seasons with "Recorded from 2013-14 only.", except a game-level count in a rate mode, which would divide by games it was not recorded in and reads blank with that reason. A slot with no games, or no sample, says so.
- **Queries:** one engine query for the three slots, plus one for the search over several seasons while it is open. On the full database, inside the worker (median of five): a 2025-26 league of players with the default rows 269 ms, the same with a date range 154 ms, last 10 games 166 ms, with tracking, play-type and impact rows 267 ms; three players over 5 seasons 199 ms, over every season 256 ms; 2025-26 teams 177 ms, three teams over every season 173 ms; the search over every season 44 ms.

### Trends

One metric by season across a player's career or a team's history in the data, 1993-94 on, against the league each season.

- **Who:** a search over every player with a game since 1993-94 ("LeBron James · LAL") or every franchise under its latest name. With nobody picked, the player or team a dashboard showed last; a choice here keeps them even when the dashboards move on. The header links to their dashboard.
- **What:** any catalogue metric read per season, by category and sub-group (D52, each under its primary one; by family before T1702.4), except a published projection (one season's state, meaningless across several). Points per game for players and net rating for teams by default.
- **Scope:** the scope bar's season type applies (regular season, playoffs and the rest); its season and range do not, since a career is every season, and the screen says so. The scope bar offers the season types of the season it shows, so the playoffs are offered once a season that has them is picked.
- **Mode:** the shared control above, shown only for a total the mode rescales.
- **The line:** a point a season. A traded player's season is one point, their whole season, with their teams named in the order they played for them. A season not played is a gap, never a line drawn across it. Before a metric is recorded the seasons read blank, never zero, and a rule marks the start with the metric's own reason ("Recorded from 2013-14."). A season below the qualifier is a hollow point with no rank, as the leaderboards leave it unranked. A lower-is-better metric runs upside down, so up is always better.
- **The league:** behind the line, each season's qualified pool: its 10th–90th and 25th–75th percentiles and its median, interpolated as DuckDB's `quantile_cont`. The pool is the leaderboard's: players the minutes qualifier admits (1,000 minutes over 82 games, scaled to that season's games) whose sample also qualifies for the metric, scaled the same way, or every team. A statistic the NBA publishes leaders in qualifies by the NBA's minimum alone (D33): 300 field goals made for FG%, 82 threes for 3P%, 125 free throws for FT%, 58 games of 82 for points, rebounds, assists, steals and blocks per game. Both rules are the engine's own `qualified` flags, read as the leaderboard's `rankWithinTab` reads them. The hover reads the band as numbers.
- **Rank:** each season in its own pool, the better side first, ties sharing a rank, the percentile as `percent_rank`: "7th of 143". On the hover and in the table under the chart, newest first, with the teams, games and minutes.
- **Notes:** where the metric starts, seasons the source lacks, why it has no answer (a published metric in the play-in), and a total for the season under way ("2026-27 is in progress."). Lockout and shortened seasons need nothing for rates.
- **Queries:** the subject by season, their teams by season, team and month, and the pool by season. A season has about 260 qualified players and the engine returns 5,000 rows at most, so the pool is asked for in parts of 14 seasons, run together. A part cut off at the limit is asked for again in halves, down to one season; a single season still cut off reads "Too many players to read the league in 2014-15: no band or rank." and has neither, rather than wrong ones. The search is one query of every player's minutes.
- **Left out from the template:** the two comparison lines, the "Career at a glance" cards, ages under the seasons and team marks on the axis (the teams are on the hover and in the table).

## Favourites

A player or a team can be starred (D49, Plan T1601). Built: the store, the star and its places (T1601.1), the mark on every screen (T1601.2), the filters (T1601.3) and the Favourites page (T1601.4). The colour on every screen, the filters and the Favourites page (T1601.2 to .4) read the same mechanism.

- **The store:** `favourites` in `metis.db` (migration `004_favourites.sql`, `src/main/db/repos/favourites.ts`), a row per star: league, kind (`player` or `team`), the id the league's stats file knows and the name as it read when starred, with the time. A star survives its league's file being deleted or rebuilt. Starring the same favourite again keeps the first star.
- **A favourite follows the person:** a linked player (D46) starred in one league of a pair reads as a favourite in the other, under their id there, named as starred and marked `from` the league where the star was given. It is resolved when read (`src/main/favourites.ts`), through the links worker's read `pairedIds` (D47: only that worker opens `links.duckdb`), so a link made by a later refresh carries the star with nothing written. The row stays where the star was given. Unstarring from either side removes the star in both leagues. With no links file, or a read that fails, a star is its league's alone and nothing errors. Teams are never linked, and a link to a draftee with no professional id has no one to star there.
- **The one renderer mechanism:** `useFavourites()` in `src/renderer/src/lib/stats/favourites.ts`, fed by `FavouritesProvider` (`screens/stats/FavouritesProvider.tsx`) above the shell in `App.tsx`, outside its per-league key. It gives the league on screen's favourite ids as two sets (`players`, `teams`), `isFavourite(kind, id)`, `toggle(kind, id, name)` and `all`, every league's favourites in one read (for the page). A toggle shows at once and is replaced by the main process's answer, the latest toggle's winning; a failed change goes back to what is stored. It reads again on a league switch, so a link made since shows on the next switch or launch. Every screen reads favourites only through it: there is no second store. Outside the provider (a screen rendered alone) it is inert and no star is drawn.
- **The star** (`components/stats/FavouriteStar.tsx`): outlined in `--fg-3` when off, filled with `--fav` when on. A button with `aria-pressed` and a label naming who it is for ("Add Jayson Tatum to favourites", "Remove Boston Celtics from favourites"), in the tab order; its click never reaches the row it sits on. Where it sits: beside the name on the player page's header and the team code on the team page's header, always shown; before the name on every row of the full Players and Teams leaderboards (`LeaderTable`), and on the player rail's ranked rows and its search's "Not ranked here" rows, in a 16 px column every row keeps, shown on the row's hover or focus while off and always while on, so no column moves and no row changes height. The rail's row is a button, so the star sits beside it over that column, never inside it. The paired league's rows in the rail's search have the column and no star: they are another league's player, starred on their own page. Everywhere else a favourite carries the static mark below.
- **The token:** `--fav`, one gold for both themes (`assets/main.css`), an addition to D17's one palette. Light `oklch(0.62 0.14 88)` (#a97f00): 3.66:1 on `--panel`, 3.59:1 on `--paper`, 3.40:1 on `--hover`, 3.15:1 on `--sel`, above WCAG's 3:1 for a graphic; yellower than `--warn` (hue 70), OKLab distance 0.074. Dark `oklch(0.79 0.16 78)` (#f2ab19): 9.36:1 on `--panel`, 9.77:1 on `--paper`, 8.88:1 on `--hover`, 7.90:1 on `--sel`; deeper and more orange than `--warn`'s pale yellow (hue 90), distance 0.074. It is for graphics alone, the star and the row edge, and never for text: 3.66:1 is enough for a mark but short of the 4.5:1 text needs, so a favourite's name keeps its own colour.
- **The mark** (T1601.2): a favourite's row carries a 2 px gold edge on its left and a small filled gold star before its name. One shared piece draws it, read only through `useFavourites()`: `favouriteEdge(on)` (`FAV_EDGE`, an inset shadow, so no row changes width or height) in `lib/stats/favourites.ts`, `FavouriteMark` (a static star, role `img`, labelled "Favourite", never a button) and `FavouriteName` (the mark before a name, the name cut where it runs long, inside the name's own cell so no column moves) beside `FavouriteStar`, and `MiniTable`'s `favourite` row flag. In a small table (`MiniTable`) the edge is a 2 px bar in an 8 px gutter left of the row (`FAV_GUTTER_EDGE`), 6 px clear of the first cell: the table and every scroll box around one (`TABLE_SCROLL`) keep that gutter with a negative margin and the same padding, so no cell moves, for a favourite row or any other. The home boards, the rail and the full leaderboards have room of their own and draw the edge inset. No screen builds its own. A row that is not a favourite gets nothing and keeps its layout; a player followed from the paired league looks like any favourite.

## Glossary

## Data panel

Every league's data, whichever league is on screen (D35), from `api.leagues.downloads()` and each league's `stats.data()` and `stats.status()`. It reads a league again as its load goes.

- **Header:** the disk the leagues' files take together, and the free space beside them.
- **One section per league,** in the registry's order: its name; a line with its state (On, Loading with files done of the total, Paused, Not enough disk space, Off, or Off: kept, not refreshed), files loaded of those published and its file's size on disk; and its buttons: Pause while it loads, Resume while paused, Turn the league on or off, and Delete its data for a league that is off and still has a file.
- **Turn off** stops the league's refreshing and keeps its stats readable; **Delete its data** asks first ("Delete the WNBA's data?", naming the file and its size) and removes the file; turning the league on again downloads it all. A league off with no file says what turning it on downloads and takes on disk, from its measured `size`.
- **Pause** stops a league's load at once and holds off any other of that league, across launches, until Resume. The step it stopped is not counted as an attempt. The other league is not paused.
- **Not enough room:** a league's first load needs its measured room free (6 GB for the NBA, D22's limit; the WNBA's in `wnba-data-sources.md`), less what is already on disk. Without it, the load does not start and its section says what it needs and what is free. Once its file is past the size of a loaded league, a refresh needs no check.
- **One row per release of the league,** grouped by load tier, in load order: its name, the seasons held, written the league's way (`1996-97` in the NBA, `1997` in the WNBA), files loaded of those published, the size as downloaded, when it was last fetched, its state. A league with no file lists its releases, none loaded.
- **State:** Loaded, Part loaded, Waiting, or Failed with the latest error still standing. A file not published yet, such as the current season's playoffs, does not count as missing. A release left out of the build (Plan T1107) reads "Not in this version", with no files expected.

## First run

On a clean profile (no choice saved and no stats file), the app asks which leagues to download before anything is fetched: each league with its full name, what it downloads, what it takes on disk and the free space it needs while it loads, the NBA ticked. Download is refused, with the reason, when nothing is chosen or when the chosen leagues together need more room than is free. The dialog cannot be dismissed without an answer. An existing profile is never asked: it keeps the NBA on and the WNBA off. The answer can be changed in the Data panel.
